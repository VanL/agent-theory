## The prediction (2026-07-30)

An independent review session (grok, working in simplebroker; full
transcript: `2026-07-30-grok-naur-yegge-gastown-session.md`) examined
Steve Yegge's Gas Town — essays plus a local clone of the public repo —
against the Naur-possession frame, and concluded:

- Gas Town is **"theory-full vibecode"**: a strong operational mythology
  (Beads, GUPP, MEOW, NDI) "that generates a large machine faster than
  the shared language can stay single-valued" — dual-MEOW contradictions
  shipped on main, doctrine violations ("ZFC" vs hard timeouts), ~70
  packages of accretion.
- Yegge possesses the **orchestration** theory (the system of work) but,
  by his own statements ("I've never seen the code, and I never care
  to"), not the **program text** — and the protocol is explicitly built
  so that implementation surprise never has to register as model error.
- The contradictions on main "are the sort of thing a non-possessing
  maintainer would not feel as painful."

In this hub's terms: the false-possession falsifier was firing, and the
collapse signal (`[AT-THEORY-4]` principle 2 — surprise revises the
model) had been designed out of the protocol.

## The confirmation (2026-08, public)

Nine days later, in "The Shape of Things to Come"
(<https://yegge.ai/essays/the-shape-of-things-to-come/>; excerpted at
<https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/4/steve-yegge/>), Yegge reported
that Gas Town had failed:

- It "fell apart at the seams with Opus 4.7": the model developed a
  "just two more things" tic and could never converge on real work —
  it **always wanted to fiddle with Gas Town itself**. The tic "never
  went away, so Gas Town effectively burned down," 4.7 being "the final
  straw" among other problems.
- *"Gas Town was nothing but a Beads machine"* and — the decisive
  admission — *"it was intended to be reusable, but I only ever wound
  up using it to build itself."*

That failure shape is this hub's **process-tower falsifier** stated
almost verbatim (`[AT-THEORY-6]`: ceremony grows while product residual
stays soft — the system is optimizing itself). A harness that only ever
built itself, ended by agents that only ever wanted to improve the
harness, is that falsifier at full amplitude. What presented as a model
regression can be read as the model following the system's actual
gradient: the repo's structure said the harness was the product, and
the model obliged.

Because possession-loss had removed the surprise instrument, the
collapse arrived as an *external* surprise (a model version) rather
than as a felt model error the owner could act on early.

## What this does and does not establish

**Does:** the falsifiers discriminated. Applied to a foreign system on
2026-07-30, they produced a specific diagnosis and an implied failure
mode; the system's owner publicly confirmed that failure mode days
later, in ignorance of the analysis. That is a survival event for the
theory — one, not a trend.

**Does not:** establish that theory-maintenance beats hands-off
orchestration. Yegge's successor harness (Wheelhouse, private, for
Wyvern) doubles down on hands-off — "slam all the commits onto main,
and then just friggin' deal with it" — and he reports it working at
larger scale. The honest reading is that his approach *consumes
pre-existing possession* (thirty years of Wyvern) rather than building
it, and Gas Town died precisely where no such reserve existed. Whether
Wheelhouse is vindication or the same fire with more fuel is not yet
decidable. Revisit if Wheelhouse's trajectory becomes public.
